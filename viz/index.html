New to order books? Start here
  • An order book is the list of everyone currently waiting to buy or sell one thing, say one company's stock.
  • Bids (blue, bottom half) are people waiting to buy, each with the most they are willing to pay. Asks (orange, top half) are people waiting to sell, each with the least they are willing to accept.
  • The best bid is the highest bid and the best ask is the lowest ask. They meet in the middle of the ladder, and the gap between them is the spread. Nobody in the book agrees on a price yet, which is why they are all waiting.
  • A trade happens when a new order is willing to meet someone already waiting: a buyer offering at least the lowest ask, or a seller accepting at most the highest bid.
  • Who trades first? The best price first, and at the same price, whoever arrived first. This is called price-time priority. Each row is a line of people waiting at one price, front of the line on the left.
  • Four kinds of message arrive: a limit order (buy or sell at this price or better, and wait if nobody matches), a market order (trade right now at any price, never wait), a cancel, and a modify (change an order's price or size).
